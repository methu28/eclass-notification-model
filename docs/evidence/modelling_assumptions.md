# Section 2.2.3: Modelling assumptions (review draft)

For each choice, the specification (PDF page 3) requires what was chosen, why it is reasonable for the inquiry, and whether it is evidence-supported or synthetic. Rationales below are proposed analytical justifications for team review, not a reconstruction of historical design conversations. Source labels refer to the [inventory](inventory.md).

1. **Twenty fixed fictional records (E6, E7).** Choice: use the same 20 inputs in all conditions, with no arrivals or removals. Rationale: hold traffic constant so reserved display capacity is the comparison variable. Status: synthetic control; no claim of a typical student list size.
2. **Constructed temporal contrast (E6).** Choice: newer nonurgent posts coexist with older deadline-bearing posts; four items begin urgent and four become urgent. Rationale: expose a possible recency/urgency trade-off and a changing urgent population in a small runnable example. Status: synthetic case, not a sampled distribution or prevalence estimate.
3. **Urgency at 0 < remaining hours <=72 (E7).** Choice: classify approaching deadlines with a fixed operational threshold. Rationale: make urgency explicit and comparable as time advances. Status: synthetic, not an official York rule; items at/past deadline are excluded from urgency. No claim that this value is optimal.
4. **Five visible list positions (E7; observed redacted E5 crop for context only).** Choice: measure ranks 1-5. Rationale: represent scarce initial display capacity with one inspectable boundary. Status: synthetic proxy; the inspected public E5 derivative retains four entry areas and does not validate five positions or a universal viewport.
5. **Recency and hybrid K=1,2,3 (E7).** Choice: baseline newest-first; primary alternative K=2, sensitivity K=1/3, promote the K newest currently urgent items then sort the rest by recency. Rationale: vary the capacity allocated to urgency while retaining recency for remaining information. Status: hypothetical policies, not recovered eClass algorithms; no urgency-only ordering added.
6. **Six six-hour updates, initial snapshot, no overdue items (E7).** Choice: record hours 0, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36. Rationale: observe the input's threshold crossings before its earliest deadline. Status: synthetic clock/horizon, not a measured notification-refresh interval or student timetable.
7. **Current recency of 24 hours and a fixed initial cohort (E7).** Choice: measure currently recent nonurgent items separately from members of the initially recent nonurgent cohort. Rationale: distinguish aging out of a window from being displaced in rank. Status: synthetic window/cohort definition, not a user-behavior finding.
8. **Visible position as opportunity, not attention (E7).** Choice: compute urgent count, coverage, mean urgent rank and nonurgent visibility only. Rationale: restrict conclusions to the process represented. Status: declared measurement proxy; no empirical reading/learning validation.
9. **Passive items and omitted delivery/behavior mechanisms (E7; E2-E4 for context).** Choice: omit student decisions, preferences, channel eligibility, subscription effects and read/dismiss state. Rationale: isolate ordering under identical records. Status: selective abstraction; relevant real-world mechanisms remain outside the computational boundary.
10. **Deterministic ID tie-breaking and recorded seed 42 (E7, E11).** Choice: use a stable identifier to resolve equal posting ages; keep the existing seed parameter. Rationale: reproducible comparisons. Status: implementation convention; no random draws occur, and repeatability does not establish statistical generalizability.

These choices let the experiment examine a visibility-allocation trade-off in one scenario. They do not establish a preferred policy for real students. The dossier keeps recency/hybrid-2 primary and the other two configurations as sensitivity conditions, not independent investigations.
